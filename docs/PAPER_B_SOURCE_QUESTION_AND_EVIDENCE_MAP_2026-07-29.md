# Paper B source questions and evidence map

## Decision

Paper B will answer the accumulated-learning question with the already-frozen
Q-R1 causal design. It will not treat the Garrido configuration matrix or a
Cobb-Douglas re-ranking as evidence of learning across disruption cycles.

## Source questions

### Garrido, Pongutá, and Adarme (2024)

The exploratory paper asks:

1. Which category of AI algorithms best mimics supply-chain learning?
2. How can that family be integrated into the internal structure of a DES for
   SCRES assessment?

These are answered empirically only if the comparison separates within-campaign
feedback from knowledge that survives a complete physical reset.

### `v.0_neuralNet-scres`

The draft asks whether neural learning integrated with the DES improves dynamic
SCRES operationalization and predictive accuracy under recurring,
heterogeneous disruptions. Its four hypotheses require:

- H1: shorter recovery;
- H2: improvement over successive disruptions;
- H3: lower performance volatility across disruption intensities;
- H4: a positive causal effect of accumulated prior learning.

The current draft contains the DES baseline but leaves the hybrid results and
findings sections empty.

### Garrido et al. (2024), factory resilience

The factory-resilience article asks which pure APP strategy yields higher
factory resilience under demand variability. It reports match-chase as the
highest-ranked pure strategy under its Cobb-Douglas index and explicitly calls
for future work combining simulation with learning algorithms under additional
risk events.

That index is a related factory-level endpoint, not the definition of retained
learning. In Paper B it may be reported only as a predeclared secondary
sensitivity after its resource and cost semantics are frozen.

## Existing evidence

- Program Q establishes within-campaign feedback value over its complete
  open-loop frontier, but not retained learning between campaigns.
- Q-R1 prospectively resets the physical system between campaigns and varies
  only retained decision-relevant knowledge.
- The Q-R1 structured confirmation shows a positive retained-information ReT
  effect but fails the frozen worst-product service guardrail.
- The factorial-v4 neural instrument is the missing architecture test. Eight
  local workers completed their paid computation but originally failed during
  packaging; their salvages were valid development artifacts but the transient
  copies are no longer present. VPS attempts then stopped at the frozen
  per-calendar comparator cap.

## Missing evidence and route

The decisive missing result is whether recurrent neural state adds value after
an explicit retained prior is already supplied, and whether any neural policy
beats the strongest tested retained structured controller with the same causal
history and resources.

The shared-structured amendment changes only where invariant comparator work is
computed. It computes the 192 frozen structured rows once, before neural
checkpoint grading, and requires every worker to load the same hash-bound
artifact. It does not change physics, information, actions, seeds, thresholds,
selection, or estimands.

The Cobb-Douglas sensitivity remains secondary until the cost/resource ledger
is defensible. No scalarized metric may retroactively select a checkpoint or
configuration.
