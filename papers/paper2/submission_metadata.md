## Abstract

Simulation-based supply-chain resilience studies often evaluate one configuration at a time and
discard information before the next discrete-event simulation run. We study the outer-loop
alternative in which search state is retained while the physical simulation and its within-run
state are reset. In a development replay, point estimates favoured state retention in all six
matched search families. A prospective expansion from 288 to 4,608 configurations then separates
transfer from repeated sampling: the prespecified factorized UCB arm exceeded both cold start and
a state-blind replay of its own marginal visits. Secondary carrier contrasts showed no corresponding
advantage for the evaluated neural carrier. We distinguish search-state retention from within-episode control, physical-chain learning,
and architecture-specific transfer. The result is conditional on the inherited demand process and
the declared reconstructed-DES contract; a bounded seasonal-demand sensitivity is treated as
development evidence and cannot regrade the confirmation. The study therefore places retained
search state before neural architecture as the first empirical question when simulation-based
resilience optimization is claimed to adapt across runs.

## Highlights

- Retained search state has positive development point estimates across six matched families.
- Prospective transfer is tested beyond a replay of the carrier's own visit marginals, and beyond a frozen ex-ante level prior.
- Factorized UCB transfers; the evaluated neural carrier does not.
- Search memory is separated from within-episode supply-chain adaptation.
